# Farseek: art direction

## Continued development

The contest has ended. The owner requested a more realistic commercial direction for the explorer and lifted the jam's code only asset and flat material restrictions. The explorer uses anatomical geometry, licensed skin and hair textures, physically based cloth and leather, continuous limb deformation and articulated feet. The established world palette and expedition identity remain the starting point, rather than a limit on detail. Asset provenance belongs in `game/assets/explorer/NOTICE.txt`, `receipts/verification-realism/` and `receipts/verification-upper-body/`.

> Sun-baked sandstone ruins of an invented cliff-carving civilisation, cut as bold chamfered masses with stepped profiles and deep incised grooves, weathered but crisp, where the only cool colour anywhere is the turquoise light of ancient technology set into lenses, channels and the gate.

Title: Farseek (first called Far Door). Every tomb holds the address of a gate; an explorer redirects sunlight with bronze mirrors to light the address glyphs, and the gate opens a live window onto another world.

## Fixed decisions

- Visible traversal hazards use depth, lighting and geometry without adding warning cards to the HUD.
- The title and the loading screen carry every world: one signature colour each (`--world-*` in `style.css`: court bronze, checkpoint lilac, isles gold, ice blue, spore lime, sea blue, vault amber), and together a warm to cool band for the logo, the bar and the ring. Neither says how many worlds there are, and the loading screen names only worlds already reached. Play's interface keeps the builders' turquoise.
- No cue depends on colour alone. HUD marks take a shape with their colour: a diamond with a light pip for a goal, an amber triangle for danger, a turquoise ring for something to use, and a dark rim on each so it reads on sunlit sand and in shade. Amber warnings painted on the ground carry inward triangles or slanted stripes drawn in their own material (`hazard-marks.js`).
- Metres. Built traversal uses 2 m cells with heights in 0.5 m steps; the forest floor uses a continuous one metre mesh. The hero is 1.75 m tall. A push block is 1.9 m on each side. A sun mirror is 1.9 m tall. A glyph stela is 2.4 m tall. The gate ring is 9 m across. The seated colossus is 14 m tall. A Warden is 1.15 m tall. The relay orb is about 0.10 m across. An address plate is 1.8 m across. A path lantern is 2.4 m tall. A floating isle is about 14 m across. A light pylon is 2.8 m tall. An ice block is 1.9 m on each side. A snow pine is about 7 m tall. A forest tree is about 16 m tall. A frost lynx is about 0.8 m at the shoulder and 1.1 m from nose to tail.
- Base at y = 0, centred on x and z, front faces +Z.
- Grass is one tuft, `meadow_grass`, 0.6 m tall, recoloured in each world: straw in the court, sage on the isles, its own moss and fern greens in the forest.
- Environment assets retain flat base colours with procedural surfaces applied at load time. Their material names use the contract's list: `plaster`, `stone`, `timber`, `tile`, `metal`, `fabric`, `foliage`, `ground`. Sandstone and basalt are `stone`, bronze is `metal`, cloth and leather are `fabric`. The explorer uses textured physical materials; `explorer.cloth` and `explorer-skin` identify its wetness response.
- Emissive turquoise parts are separate meshes with their own material (`emissive: 0x39e3d0`) so the game can switch them from dormant to lit.
- Silhouettes read at 20 m in strong sun and haze: chunky masses, stepped profiles and deep grooves, never fine filigree that disappears.

## Light and grade per world

Light and a gentle grade deepen each world's identity; the palette roles stay as above and no world gains a new colour role.

- The court's sun stands later in the afternoon (15.85 on the rig's clock): warmer, lower light, longer shadows, and distant stone shimmering with heat beyond about 25 m. The grade warms the lights and deepens the shade.
- The checkpoint stays a violet night: the grade lifts its shadows towards violet and cools its highlights towards lilac.
- The isles' dawn throws a golden bounce from the cloud sea onto every underside, and a golden rim catches Nyra's silhouette against the low sun.
- The frozen reach's aurora also hangs over the way north; clear ice and the lake's water reflect it, shimmering with the ripples and, on High, drifting with the sky.
- The Wildwood's warm shafts fall through gaps in the canopy along the sun's direction, with dust motes glinting in them.
- In the Drowned Archive the surface's caustics play over the causeway and over Nyra underwater, never inside the air shield.
- In close-ups Nyra gains a soft key, a rim in her world's light and a sharper self-shadow; the space finale keeps its own ungraded look.

## The invented civilisation

Stepped, chamfered masses; rounded medallions; crescent and disc motifs; parallel incised grooves that run like channels; masks without features except a single vertical slit. Glyphs are simple geometric shapes built as geometry: disc, crescent, twin circles, spiral, triangle with a dot, three wave lines. No text and no printed signage anywhere.

It must not read as any real culture. No pyramids, obelisks, ankhs, animal-headed figures, pharaoh headdresses, hieroglyph walls or Mesoamerican serpent heads.

## The previous expedition

Someone camped on the terrace before the explorer: a canvas tent, crates, a banner, lanterns. Timber is weathered grey-brown, canvas is patched and sun-bleached, ropes are frayed. It tells a story without words and gives the start a human scale.

## The second world: the checkpoint

The gate opens onto a bureaucratic checkpoint of the gate network, run by the Wardens: squat constructs of pale chalk stone with an oversized aged-bronze mask for a head, a glowing turquoise slit for an eye, stubby legs and an enormous customs stamp. They are comic, not frightening: exaggerated proportions, waddling, officious. Their world is night basalt and ash lilac under a violet sky, with lilac-glowing flora; the checkpoint itself is basalt with warden-chalk panels and stamp-ochre stripes. Turquoise still marks the builders' technology: eyes, lenses, lamps and the gate. Mira's field radio waits by the arrival path, the expedition's own timber case.

## The third world: the dawn isles

The checkpoint's far door opens onto a sky at dawn: pale limestone isles float over a sea of golden cloud, with more rings of the network hanging in the distance. It is the third level: the explorer follows Mira's trail from isle to isle, across bridges of turquoise light that the builders' pylons project when the held relay orb channels stored light into their lens. It stays simple and bright: warm light, soft haze, clumps of sage grass round the rims, no creatures. The builders' pylons and crystals are the same sandstone, bone limestone, bronze and turquoise as in the court; the expedition's frayed ropes and last camp are the same weathered timber, canvas and rope as on the terrace. Snapped ropes lie over the rolled rims from a piton and ring on the cap and hang free below them to frayed ends. Two intact swing ropes hang from the expedition's shear-leg gantries: two weathered timber poles leaning out over a gap, lashed where they cross, held by rope back-stays, with a bronze ring for the rope and a small vermilion strip.

## The fourth world: the frozen reach

Mira went on through a frost door: a world of ice under a twilight sky with slow ribbons of aurora, in a valley ringed by snowy peaks. Snow lies on frost-slate rock worn into layers, with snow on every ledge, icicles under the lips and waterfalls caught in ice; frozen streams and a frozen lake are clear ice over deep blue, and the ice holds blocks, spires and a stela. The only warm colours are the explorer and the expedition's timber, rope and vermilion markers; turquoise still marks the builders' technology (lenses, the ring, the stela's glyph). Ice is chunky and faceted, with bevelled edges and darker cores, never glassy filigree. Sizes: an ice block is 1.9 m on each side like the court's push block, an ice spire cluster about 4 m tall, a snow pine about 7 m, a trail cairn about 1.1 m. At the lake shore Mira brushed the snow from the ice and cut the expedition's sign into it, the banner's crescent cradling a disc, with an arrow towards the ring: scratched lines, never letters. A frozen fall that can be climbed carries Mira's scars: dark pick notches, each with a white bruise of chips below it, up a face of poured columns. Unscarred ice is never climbable. Mira's ice axes are a pair about 0.52 m tall: a weathered timber shaft, a forged steel pick and adze, explorer vermilion grip tape. They are a traversal tool, never a weapon; once taken they ride crossed on Nyra's pack and come to her fists only on scarred ice. A pale frost lynx hunts the open snow above the frozen pond, away from the frozen falls and the thin ice: silver grey with a darker spine, pale throat and belly, faint spots, barred cheek ruffs and black ear tufts, never turquoise. It stalks, goes to ground behind an ice spire, a snow-capped boulder or a pine, and crouches, wiggles and kicks up snow before it pounces on the spot where it last saw the explorer; the snow stays clear of painted rings. The orb dazzles or stuns it and a held link drags it out of cover; pistol hits draw a little blood and it falls for good, never gore.

## The fifth world: the Wildwood

The frozen ring opens onto a playable wild forest: huge old trees with buttress roots and moss, ferns, and mushrooms whose gills glow spore lime in the shade, under warm shafts of light through a high canopy. A dirt trail is part of the physical ground mesh and leads through poisonous spores, mossy stones across a sunken spore bog whose glowing water burns, and charging woodland boars to a giant tree and the castle above it. Flashing pods can be suppressed briefly by pistol fire; a relay pulse keeps them quiet longer, and every return to activity has a fresh warning. Dense trees, ferns and continuous mossy rock banks visibly bound the route, opening into wider puzzle and combat clearings. The castle's outworks share its door's mossy grey masonry; thick woody ivy over a patch of their wall marks it as climbable by hand, where the castle's own thin ivy is only dressing. Seals and the castle's address use the builders' bronze, limestone and turquoise. A stag and birds cross the avenue; pistol hits produce a brief reaction, a short red spray and a fall that settles on the ground beside a small dark pool, never gore. A forest tree is about 16 m tall with a canopy about 14 m across; a fern cluster about 1.2 m across; the tallest glowing mushroom about 1.4 m. The boars have continuous tapered anatomy, recessed nostrils, small inset eyes, curved ivory tusks, pointed ears, split hooves and short irregular coarse hair. Head lowering, pawing and low hoof dust warn of their straight charges; the dirt remains clear of painted attack lanes and rings.

## The sixth world: the Drowned Archive

The castle door opens into water above ruined masonry beneath the sea. The explorer swims down to a control desk, whose expanding translucent shield pushes the water away from a stone causeway. A turquoise grid, grazing light and moving bands make the boundary readable from both sides. Blue tint, haze and slight distortion distinguish water from clearer, warmer air inside; fish and drifting particles remain outside. A breath bar counts 45 seconds underwater, warns when low and refills in air. Steps connect the beacon's landing to the dry route between broken columns. Weathered pale stone and green bronze carry turquoise pressure seals. Currents read as pale streaks and bubbles carried along the flow, with kelp bent the same way; broken piers leave still water in their lee. The flooded stair is a roofed stairwell holding the sea between two of the builders' pressure fields, turquoise grids in turquoise frames, with an amber vent that warns before each surge. Amber marks dangerous pressure bursts and the guardian's attacks; turquoise marks a core ready for a charged pulse. The Tide Guardian reuses the builders' seated colossus, with a visible crystal core and three escalating attack phases. Fixed amber marks precede pressure shots and rising jets; the last phase adds an expanding low wave that can be jumped. The core opens between attacks: a charged orb pulse cracks it, and only pistol shots wound it. The final door opens onto space, with the gate floating without a base among nebulae, a ringed giant, an atmospheric ocean world, an ember world, moons and drifting asteroids, and a golden light form. Mira's light, the warm white of a carried lantern, comes to meet it and they circle each other while she speaks, then the two lights cross on together into the builders' vault, where the credits roll once its memory is read.

## The vault

Beyond the last door, the builders' vault is a sealed hall of the network inside a hollow rock near the drifting door. Its own air and gravity hold inside, so Nyra walks there in her body while Mira travels as a small light of lantern amber. The hall speaks the builders' language at its plainest: stepped chamfered masses of sandstone on sienna footings, bone limestone bands and lintels, basalt contrast bands, pillars cut with incised channels and medallion bosses, masks with a single vertical slit, rounded medallions with crescent notches. Turquoise stays the builders' technology: the dormant channels let into the floor, the seals, the medallions and the door's slit. Six bays open off the nave, each keeping a piece of the world it remembers, so a world's palette appears in the vault only inside its own bay: sandstone and sand for the court, night basalt and lumen lilac for the checkpoint, dawn limestone over gold cloud for the isles, frost slate and ice for the reach, moss and spore water for the wildwood, drowned stone and kelp for the archive. The sealed round door is about 14.7 m wide and 13.4 m tall, with six medallions 1.7 m across in an arch over it; a bay's seal carries a smaller medallion on a stepped plinth; a vault pillar is 7.2 m tall and a builder mask 3.3 m. Behind the door, a round memory chamber holds six masks round a well of light under a dome drawn with the network in gold points and threads, and a last door of the network whose water shows an unknown world at dusk: stepped mesas, two moons and a low gold mist. The builders appear only as gold light leaving the masks, never as figures or printed text. The bays' climbing and swinging use the journey's own materials: the frozen reach's scarred ice under Mira's picks, the castle's woody ivy, and a dark bronze cable hung from a bone limestone beam over the isles bay's chasm; the drowned passage's current shows the archive's pale streaks. Mira's light reaches the vault beside Nyra's own after their reunion in space, and inside it she speaks aloud, close and clear.

## The explorer

The explorer remains about 1.75 m tall with the established palm positions. Anatomical facial topology, textured skin, inset eyes and moving eyelids retain the accepted face. Her resting expression uses softly angled brows and neutral lips, without a fixed smile or cheek lift. The contemporary warrior direction uses a fitted sleeveless technical top, tapered charcoal and olive combat trousers, modern dark boots, a compact utility belt and streamlined back equipment. Bare shoulders and arms reveal continuous anatomy, with rounded deltoids, distinct biceps and triceps, elbow relief and subtle forearm tendons. Dense surface geometry retains shared skin weights at the armholes and fades its relief before the wrists. The supported bust flows into the rib cage beneath the fabric without separate spherical cups; an open V neckline and fitted waist shape the top. The silhouette is feminine and athletic. Fitted chest panels, curved topstitching, dark armhole binding, reinforced seam ends and zipper teeth finish the technical top. A small bevelled bronze crescent carries varied original miniature glyphs, interrupted rim engraving and restrained bronze wear. Fine alternating oval links follow the anatomical neck and chest, with a small closed clasp at the back. The connecting bail stays attached to the front link through movement. The neck joins the head with continuous shading and separate rear texture aliases; closed neck and armhole binding follows the garment surface through movement. Reinforced knee folds, stitched cargo pockets and hems articulate the trousers. Fitted dark leather, bronze eyelets, toe and heel stitching, a bound collar and raised sole edges finish the boots; their original ground contacts remain the lowest points. Coincident normals stay continuous across the leather panels. Sunglasses sit on the nose with fitted temples; fine strands enrich the existing hair. The compact pack and its visible harness follow the body surface through locomotion and swimming. Locomotion uses a controlled narrow step, responsive hips and relaxed arm carriage. Running cadence follows a 3.4 m forward stride without changing travel speed. A shoulder roll curls the torso and legs, carries weight through ground contact and unfolds into standing over 0.72 seconds. Swimming keeps rounded deltoids and a continuous shoulder surface through the complete stroke. The explorer starts without weapons. Mira's relay is recovered at the checkpoint and her twin pistols from an open case on a crate at her camp on the dawn isles. The orb stuns, staggers and cracks open weak points but never wounds; only pistol hits wound and kill. The relay learns two abilities on the way, both in the builders' turquoise: on the frost plateau, a held link becomes a taut tether that drags a light creature toward her, out of cover or out of a charge; at the first forest seal, it raises a short curved ward of segmented light in front of her that turns aside charges, spores and pressure, then needs a few seconds to gather again. Mira's relics each teach one habit with the pistols, shown in the kit and the relic reader. Acquired pistols stay visible in their holsters while the relay orb is held; holstered, carried and aimed states remain distinct. Held elbows open further during idle and walking, with the extension fading during aim and running. Realistic adventure games inform anatomy, material response and physical weight; the character remains original.

## Relics

Seven things the expeditions left behind are hidden along the way, for the curious: a rolled papyrus of the first expedition, their field radio, a page of Mira's on the isles, her scarf and her lantern in the ice, her grove sketch and the archive field note. These physical relics are small, human and worn: parchment, weathered timber, rope, leather, aged bronze and explorer vermilion, never turquoise. An eighth record is kept by a separate star stela in the court. Relaying sunlight through the three mirrors wakes its turquoise lens and makes the builders' memory readable; the gate still requires only its original three address glyphs. Every discovered record can be inspected for its account and optional clues.

A smaller checkpoint register carries three ochre glyphs and uses the existing plate's stone and crystal inlay. An optional clean stamp review earns a copy of Mira's transit file from the clerk. Its three marks are independent of the main address, and the file remains a human account in the discovery reader.

## Sound

One tune crosses every world in that world's mode and voice: the court's harp in D minor, the checkpoint's vibraphone and music box in E major, a breathy flute in G over the isles, a celesta in A minor on the ice, flute and harp in E minor in the forest, vibraphone in F under the sea, flute and celesta in C in space. Danger adds a percussion palette per world under the same harmony: hand drums in the court, wooden knocks for the Wardens, a soft frame drum and air on the isles, glassy pings on the ice, skin drums in the forest and deep pressure drums in the sea. Discoveries answer in the world's key. The isles stay as free of creature sounds as of creatures: wind, cloud hush, rope and timber. Nyra's voice is non-verbal, breath and effort only. An open door hums low with a liquid shimmer and darkens everything for a moment as she passes its membrane.

## Red lines (keep clear of existing franchises)

- The gate is a plain carved ring. Each world gives it a stepped base in its own materials (court sandstone, checkpoint basalt, isle limestone, frost slate, mossy forest masonry, drowned stone); in space it floats with no base. Its turquoise liquid membrane keeps travelling ripples, grazing reflections and a refracted live destination view on the entrance face; the far face shows the same liquid without the view. No chevrons or clamps around its rim, no rotating inner symbol ring, no dialling pedestal with symbol keys and no splashing vortex.
- The hero retains an original silhouette and equipment design, without signature costume combinations or trademarked symbols. The two modern pistols use original brushed steel, rubber grips and vermilion details. The contemporary outfit replaces the expedition jacket, scarf, goggles, satchel and bedroll.
- No names, logos or visual quotations from existing games, films or series.
- The Wardens are masked stone constructs, not goblins, orcs, gnomes or any franchise's creatures: no pointed ears, green skin or tusks.
- Health is shown as small bronze pips, not hearts; after a quiet spell out of a fight, spent pips swell back one at a time, and Story or Hard can spend half a pip. The held relay orb uses segmented bronze shells and turquoise seams, with no franchise markings. Charged, its shell turns to glass around a bright swirling core; uncharged, it is dull bronze.
