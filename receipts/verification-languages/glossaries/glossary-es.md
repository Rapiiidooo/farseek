# Spanish glossary (`es`)

Neutral international Spanish that reads naturally in Spain and Latin America. The player is addressed with `tú`; Nyra and Mira are women, so adjectives that describe them agree in the feminine (`Derribada`, `quieta`, `juntas`).

## Register and voice

| Who                              | Register                                       | Example                                                         |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Interface, hints, objective subs | `tú`, imperative                               | `Salta el hueco hasta la plataforma oeste`                      |
| Objective titles                 | infinitive (a task name)                       | `Encontrar a la primera expedición`                             |
| Interaction prompts, menu items  | infinitive                                     | `{interact} Tomar la radio`, `Reiniciar nivel`                  |
| Control names and descriptions   | infinitive                                     | `Saltar`, `Agarrar, girar, hablar, tomar`                       |
| Wardens and the clerk            | `usted`, bureaucratic stamp formulas           | `Circule.`, `Vuelva a la cola, por favor.`                      |
| Mira                             | `tú` to Nyra, warm and dry, short spoken lines | `Aquí Mira. Si cruzaste la puerta detrás de nosotros, escucha.` |
| Journal pages and relics         | field-note prose, quotes in `« »`              | `Mira escribió al margen: «Una puerta necesita…»`               |

- Prefer forms shared by both regions: preterite or present over the Spain-only perfect where the tense is free (`Dejé`, `Cruzaron`), `tomar` (never `coger`), `impulso` (not `carrerilla`), `volver a buscarlo` (not `volver a por`), no `vosotros`.
- Never use the imperative `mira` (look) or `mira` (gun sight): both collide with Mira's name. Use `orienta la vista`, `observa`, `vigila`; a gun sight is `retícula` or `puntería`.
- Never use `pulsar` for the orb's pulse: `pulsa` means press a key. The pulse is `pulso` with `lanzar` (`lanza un pulso a la lente`).
- A template that inserts a name keeps it on its own with a colon or a separator: `Preparando: {world}…`, `{action}: ahora usa {key}.`, `Examinar: {name}`.

Relic names that start with `Mira's` drop the article as titles (`Bufanda de Mira`); those that start with `The` keep it (`La radio de campaña`). Interaction prompts may shorten a relic to `de Mira` when only one is in reach (`Tomar el expediente de Mira`).

Touch buttons are 50 to 54 px circles in uppercase: `Hang` is `Colgar` (intransitive, `colgar del borde`), `Link` is `Enlace`, `Fire` is `Fuego`, `Dive` / `Rise` are `Bucear` / `Subir`. Level starts use `Tras …` (`Tras la puerta lejana`).

## Keys and devices

`Space`: Espacio, `Enter`: Enter (Spain prints Intro and Latin America Enter; Enter is understood in both), `Shift`: Mayús, `Caps Lock`: Bloq Mayús, `Backspace`: Retroceso, `Home`: Inicio; `Esc`, `Tab`, `Ctrl`, `Alt` unchanged. Sided keys read `{key} izquierda` and `{key} derecha` (a `tecla` is feminine). Mouse: ratón, `Clic izquierdo`, `Clic derecho`, `Clic central`, `Ratón 4`. Gamepad: mando, `Stick izquierdo`, `Cruceta arriba`, `Vista`, `Start`, `Botón {n}`.
